Intercognix № 0005 · entered into the Intercognix Catalogue, anno 2026

The Trespass That Becomes a Road

Why a shortcut worn across someone else's land and a slime mould's feeding tube may be obeying the same rule about how paths turn permanent.

Candidate · Unvalidated

I.The First Known Thing — the shortcut that hardens into a right

law · prescriptive easements, common law

Here is a doctrine lawyers have lived with for centuries. Cut across a neighbour's field to reach the station. Do it openly, continuously, and without anyone's permission for the statutory period — twenty years in many jurisdictions — and the common law does something strange. The trespass becomes a right. A prescriptive easement is a permanent, legally protected right of way, and it is created by nothing but persistent unauthorised use.

nobody granted it; use alone created it

The owner's title does not disappear. It simply now has a channel cut through it that they no longer have the power to close. No deed was signed, no council approved it, no payment changed hands. The path itself, walked long enough, is what generated the law. This is established doctrine, litigated in every common-law jurisdiction.

II.The Second Known Thing — the network with no plan and no brain

biology · Physarum polycephalum foraging

Now walk to a different hall of the library entirely. Physarum polycephalum, a slime mould, is a single organism with no brain, no central plan and no map. Placed on a dish, it extends an even mesh of tubes outward in every direction at once. Where a tube happens to carry more nutrient flux, it thickens; where flux runs low, the tube withers and is reabsorbed.

the channel is cut by what flows through it

The result is not a metaphor for optimisation, it is optimisation. In a well-known experiment, food sources were laid out at the positions of the towns around Tokyo, and the mould converged on a network strikingly close to the actual rail system in total length, efficiency and resilience to severed links. Nothing decided this. Flux thickened some tubes and starved others until only the good network was left. This is well-replicated experimental biology.

III.The Bridge

nobody's textbook contains both chapters

Property lawyers do not read papers on slime mould foraging. Biocomputation researchers do not read easement case law. Each fact above is old news in its own hall. But hold them in the same frame, and a shared mechanism appears:

X: usage-driven topology crystallisation

Call it usage-driven topology crystallisation. In both halls a formal structure begins indifferent to traffic — a boundary drawn on a deed, a uniform mesh of tubes — and in both, repeated unsanctioned throughput deforms the resistance landscape until the flow channel is written into the formal medium itself. Friction becomes infrastructure. And note what keeps this from being a loose analogy: both require persistence specifically. One crossing is a trespass, twenty years of crossings is a right; one pulse of nutrient does nothing, sustained flux thickens the tube. In each hall it is time under load, not the act, that does the converting.

IV.The Question That Becomes Possible

neither discipline would ask this alone

Here is the part that makes this an intercognix candidate rather than a nice metaphor. Cities already know where people actually walk and drive — the sensing problem is solved. What they lack is any mechanism for the formal layer to yield to that flux. Municipalities maintain infrastructure people have abandoned while the desire paths stay unpaved, because zoning changes on a political clock rather than a usage one. The mould has no such lag, and the common law, given twenty years, does not either.

the dynamic zoning hypothesis

If urban legal frameworks allowed property boundaries and zoning codes to mutate automatically with aggregated kinetic flux — GPS foot-traffic density — would infrastructure maintenance and municipal resource consumption settle on the same Pareto frontier that biological transport networks find?

why it would matter

If they would, then a great deal of what cities spend on roads, transit and services is the cost of a formal layer that cannot follow its own traffic — and the remedy would be procedural rather than architectural.

V.How It Could Be Proven — or Broken

a candidate must be breakable, or it is merely poetry

An intercognix candidate earns nothing until it names its own test. This one does:

What would support it

A simulated dynamic-zoning testbed shows that algorithmic easements derived from sensor flux reduce traffic bottlenecks and energy consumption substantially — the threshold worth arguing about is upward of 30 per cent — against conventional zoning-board allocation on the same city.

What would break it

Continual boundary adaptation generates game-theoretic instability: a tragedy of the dynamic commons in which actors manufacture flux to capture rights, behaviour destabilises faster than the topology can settle, and civic function degrades rather than improves.

the honest status

Until such work is done, this remains exactly what its stamp says: a candidate. A surprising structural echo between two halls of the library, waiting for someone with the tools of both to walk the corridor.
