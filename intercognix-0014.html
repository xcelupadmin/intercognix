The Store That Returns the Newest First

Why the people who design silos have known for sixty years that a store's outflow has an order — and that no measurement of its level will tell you what that order is — while pharmacokinetics still models the deep tissue depot as a vessel in which every molecule is equally likely to be the next one out.

Candidate · Unvalidated

I.The First Known Thing — the stock that never rotates

inventory rotation · stock that goes in and stock that comes out

A warehouse is a store with an inflow and an outflow, and the first thing anyone running one learns is that the two are not enough to describe it. The trade names three disciplines of rotation. First-in-first-out issues the oldest stock first and is the default wherever the goods age. First-expired-first-out issues by remaining shelf life rather than by arrival, which is not the same ordering when the consignments differ. Last-in-first-out issues whatever is nearest the door, which is whatever came in most recently, and is what a store does when nobody imposes an order on it. The three can hold the same tonnage and move the same tonnage a week. They differ in one quantity only: the age of what leaves.

in bulk storage the order is a property of the vessel

Where the goods are not in boxes the ordering stops being a matter of policy and becomes a matter of geometry. Andrew Jenike's work on bulk solids in the nineteen-sixties established that a silo discharges in one of two patterns, fixed by the wall angle and the friction between the material and the wall, and that the operator does not choose between them at the moment of discharge. In mass flow the entire contents are in motion whenever anything leaves, and the vessel is first-in-first-out. In funnel flow only a channel above the outlet moves; the material against the walls stands still, and what leaves is what was most recently tipped in on top of the channel. The standing material can remain for the life of the plant. It can cake, it can spoil, it can rathole, and it does not appear in the inventory as anything other than tonnes in the silo. Between these two patterns the level gauge, the weighbridge and the throughput meter read the same.

what the discipline does about it

Two consequences are worth carrying. The first is that a store which is topped up faster than its channel turns over can run for years while a fraction of its contents never leaves — the outflow is drawn entirely from recent deliveries, and the oldest material is not slowly diluted out but held indefinitely behind them. The second is the method. Because no measurement of the bulk distinguishes the two patterns, the discipline does not try to infer the ordering from the quantity. It marks a consignment — a lot code, a tracer, a coloured batch — and watches for when it comes out. The age distribution of the outflow is measured by labelling the inflow and by nothing else. Where the stock is genuinely indistinguishable, the marked batch is the instrument.

II.The Second Known Thing — the depot that is assumed to be stirred

pharmacokinetics · ADME and the tissue compartment

Walk to a hall with no grain in it. A drug given repeatedly accumulates in tissue and leaves it again, and the apparatus for describing this is the compartment: a volume into which drug enters at some rate and from which it leaves in proportion to how much is there. The assumption inside the word is instantaneous and complete mixing. A compartment has no interior geography and its contents have no age; the probability that a given molecule is the next to leave is the same for the one that arrived this morning and the one that arrived last year. From that assumption follows the superposition principle, which is the working basis of nearly all dose-schedule prediction: the concentration under a course of doses is the sum of the curves each dose would have produced alone, so what a patient has on board at a given moment depends on how much has been given and when, but never on the order in which the tissue received it.

the depots the field knows are not simple

The field is not naive about the tissues themselves. Bisphosphonates are known to bind to bone mineral surface, to be buried under subsequent apposition, and to return to the circulation only when remodelling excavates them, which is why their terminal half-lives are quoted in years and why the number depends on the skeleton rather than on the molecule. Lead is described in the same language: a labile trabecular pool and a slow cortical pool that gives its burden back decades later during pregnancy or bone loss. Amiodarone, the lipophilic antipsychotics and the antimalarials are understood to sit in fat and in lysosomes and to leave over months. But the descriptive vocabulary is depth and rate. The remedy, when one compartment fails to fit, is another compartment with a smaller rate constant, and the new compartment is also assumed to be well stirred.

what is therefore not asked

Linearity, when the field checks it, is checked by magnitude. The standard test compares parameters derived from a single dose with those at steady state, and asks whether exposure rises in proportion to dose; a drug that passes is declared to obey superposition. This is a test on quantities, and a store that discharges in an order but obeys its mass balance can pass it. No routine study asks which molecules are leaving. The question has no place in the apparatus, because in a well-stirred compartment the answer is fixed in advance and uninformative: in proportion to what is there.

III.The Bridge

no pharmacokinetics paper cites a hopper

The halls share nothing. Bulk handling engineers do not read clinical pharmacology, the authors of compartmental models do not read silo design, and the only literature that touches both is about how pharmaceutical warehouses should rotate their pallets. But both are describing a store that is filled repeatedly and drawn down repeatedly, and one of the two has learned that such a store has a property its level does not contain.

X: the order of withdrawal is a property the level cannot report

Call it discharge order. A store is usually described by three numbers — what goes in, what is held, what comes out — and a fourth exists that none of them fixes: the rule by which the held material is selected for release. Where the store is stirred, the rule is proportional and the age of the outflow follows from the level alone. Where the store has a geometry — where material is deposited on top of, or underneath, or behind what is already there — the rule is set by that geometry, and two stores with identical contents and identical throughput can differ without limit in the age of what leaves and in how long their oldest fraction is retained. From which follows the half that pharmacokinetics has not ported, because only the storage trades have had to answer for stock that was still in the vessel after the plant was rebuilt: where deposition builds structure, the order of withdrawal becomes a parameter in its own right, it is not recoverable from any measurement of the amount, and the only instrument that reads it is a marked consignment. A bone surface that is buried by the next month's apposition is a funnel-flow silo with a slow channel; a fat depot that is laid down and drawn on by the tissue's own turnover is a store with a rotation discipline nobody specified. In each, the compartmental model's proportional release is not an approximation to the ordering. It is the assumption that there is none.

IV.The Question That Becomes Possible

neither discipline would ask this alone

Pharmacokinetics already has the marking technology. Stable isotope labelled drug is administered routinely — to measure absolute bioavailability against an oral dose, to track release from a carrier — and the mass spectrometry that distinguishes the labelled from the unlabelled molecule is ordinary. What has not been done is to use two labels as two consignments and watch the order in which the store gives them back.

the ordering hypothesis

Does a structured tissue depot discharge in an order — so that at equal cumulative dose and equal tissue burden, the washout curve and the residual burden at a later date depend on the sequence in which the doses arrived — and can that order be read as a single index from the isotope ratio in the outflow of a cohort-labelled course?

why it would matter

Because if the order exists it is being ignored in exactly the places where the depot matters most. Superposition is what licenses the prediction that a missed month, a drug holiday, a loading dose followed by maintenance and a flat schedule all converge to the same burden and the same washout once the arithmetic of dose and interval is matched; and a last-in-first-out depot breaks that prediction in a specific direction, holding the earliest doses longest and returning the most recent first. The clinical settings where this is live are already contentious — how long the skeletal reservoir persists after a bisphosphonate is stopped, and whether the residue of a long course is comparable to the residue of a short one; how a body burden of a persistent lipophile accumulated in one period is mobilised in another. An ordering index would also sort compounds by something the current parameters do not measure. Volume of distribution reports how much tissue takes, terminal half-life how slowly it gives back, and neither reports whether the giving back is orderly. And it makes a design claim available: where the depot is layered rather than stirred, the schedule that fills it is a lever on how long the residue stays, independently of the total delivered.

V.How It Could Be Proven — or Broken

a candidate must be breakable, or it is merely poetry

An intercognix candidate earns nothing until it names its own test. This one names two, and the first is the marked consignment the storage trades would have insisted on.

What would support it

First, the cohort-labelling experiment, which needs no new technique. In an animal model with a known structured depot — a bisphosphonate in bone is the clean case, a lipophilic amine in fat the harder one — dose an accumulating course in which an early block of doses carries one stable isotope label and a late block carries another, with the labels swapped between arms to cancel any isotope effect. Then stop dosing and follow the ratio of the two labels in plasma, in urine and in serial tissue samples through washout. A stirred compartment makes a flat prediction: the ratio in the outflow equals the ratio in the store, at every time point, from the first sample to the last. Support looks like a ratio that is not flat — an early washout enriched in the late-labelled cohort and a crossover as it is exhausted, with the magnitude and the timing of the crossover reproducible across animals, larger for bone than for fat, and larger where the depositional process is faster. Second, the schedule-permutation test, which follows only if the first succeeds. Deliver the same cumulative dose in front-loaded, flat and back-loaded schedules, and compare the washout curve and the residual tissue burden at a fixed later date. Superposition predicts that these coincide once the burden at the end of dosing is matched. Support is a separation between them that the ordering index predicts in advance, in sign and in approximate size.

What would break it

The first test breaks the whole thing cleanly and could do so in a single study. If the isotope ratio in the outflow is flat within the precision of the assay — if the store hands back the two cohorts in the proportion in which it holds them — then the depot is stirred at the resolution that matters, the ordering index is zero, and there is nothing here that the existing compartment did not already say. It breaks a second way if the ratio does move but the movement is reproduced by a conventional linear model with one further compartment: if a nested fit shows that a three-compartment model with an extra slow term accounts for the label trajectories as well as the ordering model does, by any honest criterion for the added parameters, then the ordering is a re-description of compartmental depth and the index collapses into a rate constant the field already fits. It breaks a third way if the separation is an artefact of the marking: if a deuterium label alters the rate of metabolism, or if the two labels differ in assay drift over the months of a washout study, the crossover can be manufactured without any geography in the tissue, and the label-swapped arms are what would reveal it.

the honest status

Note the narrowness. The claim concerns drugs whose depot is built by a depositional process — mineral apposition, lipid deposition, the growth of keratinised tissue — and not the well-perfused tissues, where mixing is fast and where the stirred compartment is very probably exact. It is a claim about the order of release and about nothing else: it does not say that any reported volume or clearance is wrong, it says that a quantity exists which those do not report and which has not been measured. It does not predict that ordering is common; funnel flow requires a geometry, and most of the body has none. And the whole thing may be a category error in soft tissue, where a depot that looks layered at the scale of a bone surface may be thoroughly mixed at the scale of a lipid droplet turning over in days. Until a marked consignment has been put in and watched for, this remains exactly what its stamp says: a candidate. A structural echo between two halls of the library, waiting for someone with the tools of both to walk the corridor.
